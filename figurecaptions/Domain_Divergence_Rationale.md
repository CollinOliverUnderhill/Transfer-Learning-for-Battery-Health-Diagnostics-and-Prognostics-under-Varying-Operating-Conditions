# Domain Divergence Rationale for the Thesis Split

## 1. Why this note exists

This note records the rationale behind the source-target split used in this thesis, especially in response to the possible question:

> Why can a lifetime-ranked split be interpreted as a domain-divergence setting?

The key point is that the thesis should not claim that a lifetime split automatically proves domain divergence. Instead, the lifetime-ranked protocol is a task-oriented way to construct a practically meaningful transfer setting, whose domain-divergence character must be justified by the coupled effects of operating conditions, feature distributions, and predictive behavior.

## 2. How the reference paper defined out-of-distribution

In the paper *Predicting battery lifetime under varying usage conditions from early aging data*, the out-of-distribution setting was defined explicitly by **DoD region**:

- the data were partitioned at the **group level**
- **40% DoD** was used as the boundary
- groups in the **high-DoD region** were used for training and in-distribution testing
- groups in the **low-DoD region (<40% DoD)** were used as the **out-of-distribution / extrapolation test set**

Therefore, the reference paper used a **single stress-factor axis** to define OOD, and its logic was:

- DoD is a dominant stress factor
- shifting to a different DoD region creates a meaningful extrapolation setting

## 3. How the thesis split was constructed

The thesis does **not** define source and target domains by a direct DoD threshold.

Instead, the split was constructed in a **group-level lifetime-ranked manner**:

1. usable cells at a given feature week are collected
2. cells are grouped by `group_num`
3. each group is summarized by lifetime statistics, especially `lifetime_median`
4. groups are sorted by lifetime rank
5. the earlier-ranked groups form the **source domain**
6. the later-ranked groups form the **target domain**
7. within the target domain, a subset of cells is selected for **target fine-tuning**, while the remaining cells are held out as **target-test**

For the main `w5 targetspread` protocol, the split summary is:

- usable cells: 231
- source/train cells: 167
- target fine-tuning cells: 35
- target-test cells: 29
- source groups: 43
- target groups: 18

Within the target domain, the fine-tuning cells were not intended to collapse into a narrow corner. The design aimed to preserve target-domain coverage and keep truly held-out target-test cells for evaluation.

## 4. Why lifetime ranking alone is not enough

A reviewer can reasonably question the split by saying:

> Lifetime is the label. Why does splitting by lifetime imply domain divergence?

This criticism is valid if the thesis were to argue that:

> lifetime difference itself equals domain divergence

That statement would be too strong.

Lifetime is an outcome variable, not a direct domain variable. Therefore, **the thesis should not present lifetime ranking itself as sufficient proof of domain divergence**.

## 5. Why the split can still be defended

The split can be defended when it is framed more carefully.

### 5.1 A DoD-only partition does not fully cover domain divergence

Using DoD alone is clear and interpretable, but it mainly captures **one stress dimension**.

In this dataset, battery degradation is not governed by DoD alone. It is shaped by the coupled effects of:

- charging C-rate
- discharging C-rate
- depth of discharge

If domain divergence arises from the combined effect of these three operating-condition factors, then a DoD-only split may capture only part of the practical shift.

### 5.2 Lifetime can act as a practical proxy for coupled degradation severity

The thesis has already shown that **DoD and lifetime are strongly correlated**.

This matters because it means lifetime variation is not arbitrary noise. Rather, lifetime partly reflects the stress burden imposed by operating conditions.

More importantly, lifetime is not only linked to DoD, but can also reflect the **combined outcome** of:

- charging rate
- discharging rate
- DoD
- and their interaction

Therefore, a group-level lifetime ranking may serve as a **task-oriented proxy** for the composite degradation severity generated by multiple operating-condition factors.

### 5.3 The split should be described as condition-linked and task-oriented

The most defensible wording is:

- the split is **not a purely label-based partition**
- it is a **condition-linked, task-oriented protocol**
- it was designed to induce a source-target gap that may reflect the combined effect of multiple operating-condition factors

This is weaker and more honest than claiming that lifetime itself defines domain divergence, but it is also more defensible.

## 6. Recommended thesis position

The thesis should avoid saying:

> Splitting by lifetime directly proves domain divergence.

Instead, the thesis should say something closer to:

> A DoD-only partition may capture only one dominant stress dimension, whereas the group-level lifetime ranking used in this thesis was intended to reflect the combined effect of charging rate, discharging rate, and DoD on degradation severity. The resulting source-target split is therefore task-oriented and condition-linked, rather than a purely label-based stratification, and may better represent the composite domain divergence induced by multiple operating-condition factors.

## 7. Strongest defense logic

The most robust defense is a four-step logic:

1. **DoD is strongly associated with lifetime**, so lifetime is not arbitrary with respect to cycling stress.
2. **DoD alone does not fully cover the practical domain shift**, because degradation is jointly influenced by charging rate, discharging rate, and DoD.
3. **A lifetime-ranked group split can therefore act as a practical proxy** for the coupled effect of multiple operating-condition factors on degradation severity.
4. The final claim of domain divergence should be supported not only by the split rule itself, but also by:
   - operating-condition distribution differences
   - feature-distribution differences
   - performance gaps between source-only and transfer settings

## 8. A concise English paragraph for later use

The following wording is suitable for later adaptation into the thesis:

> Unlike the reference study, which defined out-of-distribution cells explicitly by a DoD threshold, the present thesis adopted a group-level lifetime-ranked source-target partition. This choice was not intended to equate lifetime itself with domain membership. Rather, it was motivated by the observation that battery lifetime in this dataset is strongly associated with cycling stress and reflects the coupled effects of charging rate, discharging rate, and DoD. In that sense, the lifetime-ranked partition serves as a task-oriented and condition-linked proxy for degradation severity, and may better capture the composite domain divergence induced by multiple operating-condition factors than a single-axis DoD split.

## 9. Final takeaway

The safest overall position is:

- the thesis split is **not** justified simply because it uses lifetime
- it is justified because lifetime here is linked to operating-condition-driven degradation
- and because a single DoD axis may be too narrow to represent the full domain shift
- so the lifetime-ranked split is best understood as a **practical, task-oriented proxy for multi-factor domain divergence**
